# Formatting specification

Contents: 1 Paper and length · 2 Language and writing style · 3 Typography · 4 Margins · 5 Header and footer · 6 Page numbering · 7 Heading numbering · 8 Tables, figures, equations · 9 Abbreviations · 10 Software output · 11 Printing, copies, delivery

## 1. Paper and length

- Plain white A4 paper. Ready-made covers that contain colors, engravings, or drawings are not used, even for the main cover, so that the document takes the standard academic form.
- Minimum **80 pages**. The following are **not** counted toward the 80: title page, table of contents, abstract, list of abbreviations/symbols, list of references, list of tables/figures, glossary, appendices.
- Page counting starts from the cover page of the first chapter (or part). That cover page is counted in the numbering but its number is not printed. Numbering runs to the end of the recommendations page.
- Avoid large blank areas, except at the end of a chapter. If a table does not fit in the remaining space, split it and continue on the next page. Figures should be resized until they fit the remaining space.

## 2. Language and writing style

- Write in Arabic or English; all parts of the document must use the same language.
- Style must be scientific and standard: state the problem under study clearly and precisely, order relevant points logically, and avoid unnecessary digressions.
- Write so that it is understandable to specialists and to readers with general knowledge of the field. Use a word processor; text, charts, and tables are produced with software.
- After final proofreading, convert to **PDF** so fonts and layout stay fixed on any computer.
- Scientific terms in Arabic text are written in Arabic with the English equivalent in parentheses, following English capitalization rules (e.g., capital first letter where applicable).
- English references stay in English (see `references-format.md`).
- Printing: line spacing **1.5** with automatic space after each paragraph. Text is **justified** (both edges aligned).
- Main chapter/section headings may be centered, or aligned right (Arabic) / left (English). Subheadings are never centered.
- Avoid hyphenating or splitting a word, phrase, or paragraph across lines unless unavoidable. Do not stretch or distort words.
- The document title (project name) is written on the main cover page in the same language as the rest of the document. The student's enrollment number may follow the name for bachelor projects. A college or institution logo may be placed on the cover.

## 3. Typography

Use the same font for the main text of the whole document: **Times New Roman** (the original guide specifies Arabic Transparent; this skill is configured to use Times New Roman instead — switch back only if the user asks).

| Element | Font | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Heading 1 (chapter title, level 1) | Times New Roman | 18 pt |
| Heading 2 | Times New Roman | 16 pt |
| Heading 3 | Times New Roman | 14 pt |
| Heading 4 | Times New Roman | 12 pt |
| Heading 5 | Times New Roman | 12 pt |
| Body text | Times New Roman | 12 pt |

- Bold or italic may be used to highlight any word or phrase, provided the formatting does not extend over more than a full paragraph or a page.

## 4. Margins

- **Arabic text**: top 2.5 cm, bottom 2.5 cm, right 4 cm, left 2 cm.
- **English text**: same top/bottom; left and right margins are swapped (left 4 cm, right 2 cm).

## 5. Header and footer

- **Header**: chapter title and chapter number. In Arabic layout, the chapter title is at the left and the chapter number (Chapter One, Chapter Two, …) at the right.
- **Footer**: page number only, centered, with no symbols, brackets, or text around it.
- Each chapter begins on a new page. The first page of each chapter shows, at the top, the chapter number (far right in Arabic) and chapter title (far left).

## 6. Page numbering

- Front matter, from after the cover up to the start of the first chapter/part: Roman numerals (i, ii, iii, iv, …).
- Body, from the first chapter's cover page to the end: Arabic numerals 1, 2, 3, …; the first page is counted but its number is not printed.
- Numbering is continuous across the whole project; it must not restart for each chapter.

## 7. Heading numbering

- The project consists of chapters (or parts), subdivided into sections; number of sections is project-dependent, but numbering must be sequential.
- Chapter number: Chapter 1, Chapter 2, ….
- Section numbering is hierarchical: 1.1 (first section of Chapter 1), 1.2.3, and so on. Do not go deeper than level 4 (e.g., 1.2.3.4).
- A main heading's number = chapter number + sequence (e.g., 1.1 Introduction). A side heading (sub-heading) adds further levels (e.g., 1.2.4 Definitions = fourth side heading under the second main heading of Chapter 1).

## 8. Tables, figures, equations

**Captioning and numbering**
- Every table, picture, or chart gets a number and a caption so it can be referenced in the text unambiguously.
- Numbering is sequential, includes the chapter number, and runs through the whole document: Table 1.1 – Encryption codes; Figure 5.2 – Bar chart of results.
- The caption is short and descriptive. Do not use the word "illustrates"/"يوضح" in any caption.
- Table captions go **above** the table; figure/picture captions go **below** the figure. Captions use the same font as the main text.
- In the text, refer to them by number: "Table (2.7)" refers to the 7th table in Chapter 2.
- If a table continues on the next page, do not repeat the full caption; repeat only the header row and the table content, with a note such as "(Table 2-7 continued)".
- The list of tables is titled "List of Tables" (فهرس الجداول); the list of figures uses the same style with the word "Figure" (شكل) replacing "Table" (جدول).

**Equations**
- Equations that contain Latin or Greek letters are written left to right as they normally appear.
- Each equation gets a sequential number placed at the end of its line, so it can be referenced by number in the text. Example: `A = πr² ........ (1)`

## 9. Abbreviations

- Abbreviations are allowed in the body after the full name has been used the first time.
- Write abbreviation letters in capital letters.
- Collect them in the **List of Abbreviations** (قائمة المختصرات) with three columns: abbreviation, full name, meaning/definition. Example row: `ASCII | The American Standard Code for Information Interchange | the American standard code for exchanging information between electronic devices`; `CPU | Central Processing Unit | the central processing unit in computers`.

## 10. Software output

- If source code, program output, or diagrams produced with specific software must be included, put them in the appendix or the relevant diagrams section.
- Screenshots can be placed on white paper of the same type, respecting the same margins; print must be clear.

## 11. Printing, copies, and delivery

- The number of copies is set by the department; the department head announces it and notifies the student when due.
- Final copies are printed after the project is defended and the committee's corrections/notes are applied. Copies handed to the department must be clear, free of spelling and scientific errors, with **no strike-throughs or erasures**, and bound (hard cover) in **black**.
- Copies submitted for the committee before the defense are not bound.
- In all cases the student is responsible for the document's conformity with the guide. The department does not accept or permit a defense until all requirements have been verified.
